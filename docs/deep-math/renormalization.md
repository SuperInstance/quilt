### The Renormalization Group: A Brief Formalism

In statistical mechanics and quantum field theory, the Renormalization Group is a mathematical apparatus used to study the changes of physical systems as viewed at different scales. The core idea, pioneered by Kenneth Wilson, is that a physical system can be described by an effective Hamiltonian, $H$, which encodes the energy dynamics and interactions of the system. 

An RG step consists of three primary actions. First, *coarse-graining*: the microscopic degrees of freedom (e.g., individual atomic spins or local fluctuations) are averaged out or "integrated out" over small regions of space. Second, *rescaling*: the lengths in the system are rescaled by a factor $b$ ($x' = x/b$) so that the system looks the same size as before. Third, *renormalization of parameters*: the coupling constants (like temperature, magnetic field, or interaction strengths) in the Hamiltonian are adjusted so that the new Hamiltonian, $H'$, describes the same macroscopic physics. 

If a system is at a critical point, it possesses scale invariance. Under an RG step, a scale-invariant Hamiltonian remains unchanged; it is an RG fixed point. The RG flow maps the space of all possible Hamiltonians, tracing how microscopic parameters evolve into macroscopic ones.
